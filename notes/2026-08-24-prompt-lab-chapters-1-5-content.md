## Chapter 1 — The Blank Box
**Kills:** Doesn't know what to ask · **Fix:** Start from your task, not from the AI

**The mistake:** "Help me with this patient." *(typed into an AI tool, met with a vague, stalling reply)*

**Why it failed:**
- No task named — the model has nothing to act on.
- No case details — nothing to reason from.
- Treats the AI like it should read your mind, the way a search bar can't either.

**The fix:** **Before you open the box, finish this sentence: "I need it to ___ (task) using ___ (this data), so I can ___ (my goal)."**

**Before / After**
- Weak prompt: `Help me with this patient.`
- Weak output: *"I'd be happy to help! Could you tell me more about the patient — their symptoms, history, and what you need help with?"*
- Strong prompt: `You are advising a GP in an OPD. 45M, diabetic, 3-day fever with productive cough, SpO2 96%. List the top 3 likely diagnoses and one red flag to check for each. Bullet list, under 80 words.`
- Strong output: *"1. Community-acquired pneumonia — red flag: SpO2 <94% or breathlessness. 2. Diabetic ketoacidosis — red flag: fruity breath, high glucose. 3. Viral URI with secondary infection — red flag: fever >5 days. Advise chest X-ray and blood glucose check before treatment."*

**Takeaway card:** *"If you don't know what to ask, you're not ready to ask it yet — start from the task."*

## Chapter 2 — Be Specific
**Kills:** Vague prompts → generic answers · **Fix:** Add case detail, numbers, constraints

**The mistake:** "What could be causing chest pain in a 60-year-old?"

**Why it failed:**
- No history, vitals, or risk factors — the model returns a textbook list, not a ranked read for this patient.
- No urgency signal — nothing tells it this might be time-critical.
- No output shape requested — the answer sprawls instead of ranking.

**The fix:** **Add the numbers and constraints that make this patient *this* patient — age, key history, vitals, duration — and ask for a ranked, capped answer.**

**Before / After**
- Weak prompt: `What could be causing chest pain in a 60-year-old?`
- Weak output: *"Chest pain in a 60-year-old could be due to many causes including cardiac issues like angina or myocardial infarction, gastrointestinal causes like GERD, musculoskeletal pain, anxiety, pulmonary causes like pulmonary embolism, and others. A full history and examination would be needed to narrow this down."*
- Strong prompt: `You are advising a GP. 60F, smoker, HTN, 2h central crushing chest pain radiating to jaw, BP 150/95. Rank the top 3 differentials with the immediate action for each. Bullet list, under 120 words.`
- Strong output: *"1. Acute coronary syndrome — ECG + troponin now, aspirin if not contraindicated. 2. Aortic dissection — check BP in both arms, urgent CT if suspected. 3. Unstable angina — same urgent workup, admit for monitoring. Given the risk factors and radiation pattern, treat as cardiac emergency until ruled out."*

**Takeaway card:** *"A textbook list isn't a diagnosis. Numbers and constraints turn it into one."*

## Chapter 3 — Give It a Role
**Kills:** No framing, so AI picks its own · **Fix:** "You are a physician advising a GP…"

**The mistake:** "Explain diabetic ketoacidosis." *(no role/audience — the depth and voice are unpredictable, run to run)*

**Why it failed:**
- No persona set — the model defaults to a generic, average-reader voice.
- No audience named — depth and vocabulary are a guess.
- The result varies run to run because nothing anchors "who's asking, who's this for."

**The fix:** **Name who the model is and who it's writing for — "You are a physician advising a GP" — before anything else.**

**Before / After**
- Weak prompt: `Explain diabetic ketoacidosis.`
- Weak output: *"Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is a serious complication of diabetes that occurs when the body produces high levels of blood acids called ketones..." (a generic encyclopedia-style entry, mixing patient- and clinician-level detail)*
- Strong prompt: `You are a physician advising a covering GP overnight. Explain DKA management priorities in the first hour — assume they know the basics, focus on what to do now. Bullet list, under 100 words.`
- Strong output: *"1. IV fluids first — 0.9% saline bolus. 2. Check K+ before starting insulin; correct if <3.3. 3. Fixed-rate IV insulin infusion, not bolus. 4. Hourly glucose + ketone monitoring. 5. Identify and treat the trigger (infection, missed insulin)."*

**Takeaway card:** *"No role means the model picks one for you. Name it, or it names itself."*

## Chapter 4 — Say the Task Out Loud
**Kills:** Topic given instead of task · **Fix:** Verbs: rank, draft, summarise, compare

**The mistake:** "Diabetes management." *(a topic, not an instruction — the model has to guess what to do with it)*

**Why it failed:**
- A topic isn't an instruction — the model doesn't know if you want a summary, a comparison, a draft, or a quiz.
- No verb means no defined output shape.
- The model picks a default (usually an overview), which may not be what you needed.

**The fix:** **Open with a verb — rank, draft, summarise, compare, flag — that names exactly what you want done.**

**Before / After**
- Weak prompt: `Diabetes management.`
- Weak output: *"Diabetes management involves several key components including blood glucose monitoring, medication adherence, dietary changes, regular exercise, and routine follow-up visits..." (a generic overview, not action-shaped)*
- Strong prompt: `Compare metformin vs. SGLT2 inhibitors as first-line therapy for a newly diagnosed type 2 diabetic with mild CKD. Table format: mechanism, key benefit, key caution. Under 100 words.`
- Strong output: *A 3-row table — Metformin (reduces hepatic glucose output / cheap, well-established / avoid if eGFR <30) vs. SGLT2i (blocks renal glucose reabsorption / renal + cardiac protection / risk of DKA, UTIs).*

**Takeaway card:** *"A topic tells the model what to think about. A verb tells it what to do."*

## Chapter 5 — Control the Format
**Kills:** Wall-of-text output · **Fix:** Length, structure, tone, citation style

**The mistake:** "Summarise this patient's history." *(no format given → a dense paragraph, not something scannable on a busy ward)*

**Why it failed:**
- No length limit — the model defaults to being thorough over being scannable.
- No structure requested — a wall of text instead of bullets or a table.
- No tone/audience constraint — reads like a textbook, not a quick handover.

**The fix:** **Always specify the shape: length, structure (bullets/table), tone, and — if it cites anything — the citation style and a note to flag unverifiable claims.**

**Before / After**
- Weak prompt: `Summarise this patient's history.`
- Weak output: *One long unbroken paragraph running through history, meds, and labs with no breaks.*
- Strong prompt: `Summarise this patient's history in 5 bullets for a handover note: active problems, current medications, key recent results, allergies, pending items. Plain language, under 80 words.`
- Strong output: *Five clean bullets — Active problems / Meds / Recent results / Allergies / Pending — each one line.*

**Takeaway card:** *"If you didn't ask for a shape, you get a paragraph. Ask for the shape you'll actually use."*
